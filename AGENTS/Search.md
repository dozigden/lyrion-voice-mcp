# Search Guidance

Read this before changing search contracts, ranking, observation capture, catalogue ingestion, or search storage.

- Production artist, album, and track search uses the catalogue-backed `catalogue-phuzzy-sqlite` resolver version 6. Version 6 carries native LMS rating, effective track year, canonical album year, direct canonical genre membership, and canonical track-to-artist and album-to-album-artist identities. It supports rating, exact-genre, and inclusive-year filtering plus rating browse. `ProductionCatalogueSearchService.Descriptor` is the authoritative production resolver identity; resolver and index-builder consumers must read the shared descriptor rather than repeat its name or version. Playlist discovery remains an isolated LMS `playlists` request for unconstrained searches.
- Keep resolver, candidate, execution, metric, and diagnostic contracts in Search production-neutral. The Evaluation executable consumes those contracts for benchmarking; deployed code must not depend on Evaluation types or the executable project.
- Return separate result groups with internal caps of 5 artists, 5 albums, 5 top tracks, 30 tracks, and 5 playlists. Top tracks must satisfy the effective search rating constraint and native rating 80 or above. The ordinary track pool still includes highly rated tracks, but prepare 35 ordinary candidates, remove selected top-track identities at the end, and then take 30 so the public groups do not overlap. Preserve LMS order within the playlist group. Keep the caps central and internal so they can be tuned without adding caller-selected limits or pagination.
- The public MCP search text field is optional `name`, not `query`, to signal that it accepts only artist, album, track, or playlist name text. Omitted, empty, and whitespace-only names are equivalent: combine them with rating, one exact genre, and/or a complete year range for filtered discovery, or omit every input for broad varied track discovery. A year range without rating or genre may return albums and tracks; do not add a result-kind selector. Reject supplied non-blank names with no normalised letter-or-digit token, over 500 characters, over 20 such tokens, or recognisable numeric rating syntax before starting retrieval. Correct misplaced rating syntax towards the separate `rating` and `ratingMatch` fields while continuing to allow ordinary numbered media names. Treat `*` as invalid rather than a wildcard. Keep diagnostic and production limits aligned where they share validation.
- The production resolver uses bounded normalised, compact, acronym, consonant-skeleton, Double Metaphone, token/prefix, and trigram retrieval lanes, then applies the application-owned scorer. Preserve the distinction between retrieval evidence, ranking score, and confidence; no confidence or speculative no-match threshold is implemented.
- Track selection preserves descending relevance score bands, varies candidates only within an equal-score band, and spreads albums round-robin within that band. Name-free discovery streams every qualifying catalogue track through bounded 200-item regular and top-track reservoirs; a year-only search also streams qualifying canonical albums through a separate bounded 200-item reservoir and varies up to 5. An empty constraint makes discovery broad rather than acting as a textual wildcard and remains track-only. Top-track variation uses a soft positive rating weight within a relevance band: native 80 has weight 1, 90 weight 2, and 100 weight 3, with intermediate native ratings interpolated. Never sort top tracks strictly by rating or give an eligible four-star track zero probability. Use the same injectable selection algorithm for top and ordinary tracks so tests can remain deterministic while production searches rotate naturally.
- A unique exact whole-query artist match expands tracks and album previews through canonical artist relationships stored in the published index and is exposed separately as required nullable `exactArtistMatch`; keep the ordinary `artists` list empty on this route. For unconstrained exact searches, stream the complete album-artist relationship into a bounded 200-item reservoir and vary up to 5 albums from it. Pin each eligible aligned exact self-titled album into that preview before varying the remaining slots, excluding pinned identities from the reservoir so they cannot duplicate. A year-only constraint applies the canonical album year inside the relationship before pinning, so an out-of-range self-titled album is not returned. Exclude track-artist-only guest appearances and leave ordinary album search unchanged. Its `discographyBrowseRef` must browse the complete unfiltered album-artist discography, preserving the exact candidate correlation through descendants. An exact self-titled album is coherent with this route only when its single canonical album-artist identity equals the exact artist identity; retain it through the discography relationship rather than treating it as a competing match. Do not infer alignment from display artist text. Do not resolve when another artist has the same exact title, an exact track exists, or any exact album has a missing, multiple, or different canonical album-artist identity. Determine this identity interpretation from the unconstrained whole-query candidates before applying requested filters, so a year filter may omit the self-titled album preview without losing the artist interpretation and rating or genre filters may return the interpretation with no qualifying tracks. Never use filters to hide a genuine exact-media conflict. Fuzzy artist matches remain on ordinary search retrieval. Stream the artist's complete track relationship once into separate bounded 200-item ordinary and top-track reservoirs rather than accepting the retrieval lanes' artificial subset or materialising a large library. Genre or rating constraints make exact searches track-only even when a year is also supplied; never ignore them to produce album previews. A future explicit artist-reference route may reuse this identity selector, but it is not implemented.
- Numeric tokens must contribute to phonetic evidence through spoken digit forms. Do not allow a phonetic encoder to silently discard a digit while treating the remaining words as a complete query span.
- Keep the public result contract independent of catalogue rows, SQLite documents, and LMS response shapes.
- The catalogue projection and disposable index retain required integer 0–100 track ratings internally. Only `lms-core` statistics feed this field; LMS missing and zero ratings both become native zero. Public MCP and observation mappings divide native values by 20 and expose decimal 0–5 numbers.
- Rating, genre, and year constraints are conjunctive and skip playlist retrieval. Tracks must satisfy every supplied constraint: genre is one trimmed, case-insensitive exact canonical genre, years use `track.Year ?? album.Year`, and ratings use the native track rating. Albums are eligible only when the complete constraint set consists of a year range, optionally with name text; filter them by canonical album year and never infer eligibility from member track years. Rating or genre makes the complete request track-only, including when a year is also present; never ignore or relax either to produce albums. Unknown genres return no tracks, duplicate canonical genre rows with the same name form a union, and missing direct track membership does not infer from album or artist. Years are inclusive and missing years do not match. Require both year bounds, normalise 0–99 shorthand relative to the current UTC year, accept full years from 1000 through next year, and swap reversed effective bounds. `exact` rating matches only the corresponding native integer; a public decimal with no exact native representation matches nothing. `at_least` uses the smallest native integer at or above the public decimal. In particular, exact 0 selects native zero while at-least 0 selects every track. Apply every constraint inside every SQLite retrieval lane before its candidate limit, not after retrieval or ranking.
- Rating browse exposes floor buckets 0 through 5: native 0–19, 20–39, 40–59, 60–79, 80–99, and 100. Within a bucket order by native rating descending, then title, artist, album, and stable identity. Keep paging opaque at the MCP boundary.
- Search returns one opaque result handle per candidate; it does not return a separate public search identifier. Each handle combines candidate correlation with underlying LMS identity and is exposed as `browseRef`, `playRef`, or both according to capability.
- Returning the same media item from two searches must produce distinct result references for the two candidate occurrences.
- Artist, album, and playlist `browseRef` values can seed `browse`. Preserve correlation through derived browse descendants and continuations so successful playback or queue mutation marks the originating result selected. Artists and album artists are never directly playable.
- A production index is disposable derived data. Build only from storage-neutral `ICatalogueSearchDocumentSource` batches of 500 or fewer documents, including canonical album-artist identities for albums plus main-track artist identities and album-artist fallback identities for tracks; never materialise the complete library in application memory.
- Build in a job-specific staging generation, validate it, move it into place, and atomically replace the small current-generation pointer. Continue serving the previous compatible generation throughout a rebuild.
- If no compatible published generation exists, return an explicit unavailable error. Do not build lazily and do not fall back to LMS artist, album, or track search.
- Startup readiness may enqueue a durable production index rebuild when the current successful catalogue has no compatible artifact for the same refresh. This remains background job orchestration rather than a lazy search-path build, does not contact LMS, and defers while catalogue refresh work is active.
- A successful catalogue refresh queues exactly one production index rebuild. Manual rebuild is a REST/UI administration action, not an MCP tool.
- The EF-backed operational observation store records original and trimmed query, optional rating and genre constraints, requested and effective year bounds, resolver/version, retrieval sources, timings, ordered candidates with public ratings, explicit named/name-free-filtered/broad interpretation, explicit exact-artist interpretation, zero-result searches, failures, later successful selections, and human reviews. Record unconstrained, requested-constraint, top-constraint, complete filtered-track, complete filtered-album, complete broad-track, exact-artist track, and exact-artist album catalogue retrieval legs separately when they occur, including per-leg status, duration, and result count. A mixed album-and-track year request has no scalar requested kind; track-only constrained and broad requests retain track. Preserve interpretations, exact-artist markers, and constraints through persistence and the administration API so the observation UI labels them without inference. Services owns its scopes, model/entity mapping, and retention; EF repositories own its queries.
- `SearchObservationRecorder` owns observation lifecycle metadata, DTO construction, source evidence, and best-effort persistence. Keep `SearchService` focused on validation, concurrent retrieval, candidate ordering, correlation, references, and the public outcome.
- If playlist retrieval fails, retain catalogue candidates and per-source failure evidence while failing the public search call. Treat failed searches separately from completed searches with no results.
- Observation recording is best effort and must not fail search, playback, or queue operations.
- Only mark candidates selected after their LMS mutation succeeds. Never mark skipped, failed, or unattempted batch items selected.
- Evaluation exports contain only explicitly included cases and omit observation IDs, LMS media IDs, correlation references, timestamps, and private notes. The canonical real corpus lives only in the private sibling repository.
- Spotify may be used only in a later offline experiment over recorded misses. It is not a runtime fallback or dependency.
- `GET /api/evaluation` and `POST /api/evaluation/search` expose diagnostics for the actual production resolver under the external name `production`, including named constrained retrieval and a bounded `constraint_scan` lane for name-free rating/genre/year and broad diagnostics. Year-only diagnostics include canonical albums and effective-year tracks; rating or genre diagnostics remain track-only. Keep lane and scoring evidence available without accepting or persisting corpus cases.
- Retired comparator implementations do not belong in production or Evaluation.

## Decision rationale

- Private evaluation compared a lexical baseline, full-scan phuzzy scoring, bounded SQLite retrieval lanes, shared-scoring Lucene lanes, and native Lucene ranking. The bounded SQLite lane resolver retained the relevant observed quality while materially reducing query latency against both the full scan and Lucene variants, so it became the first production engine.
- The comparator measurements are historical private evidence, not a general quality claim. Continue evaluating the published production artifact against the private corpus; do not restore retired comparators merely to preserve their history.
- Keep the application database, rebuildable search artifact, and application-owned resolver as separate boundaries even though the first two currently use SQLite. Backend retrieval scores are ranking evidence, not confidence or probabilities.
- Lucene remains only as the source of Double Metaphone analysis. Numeric tokens must be expanded to spoken forms before phonetic encoding, and complete spans must remain distinct from pooled token codes so ignored tokens cannot masquerade as complete phonetic evidence.
- The primary Home Assistant path supplies recognised text rather than N-best alternatives or recognition confidence. Do not make runtime search depend on either signal unless the caller contract changes.
- Preserve aliases, pronunciations, and reviewed corrections as portable application-owned data when implemented; do not bury them in backend-specific tokenizer configuration.
